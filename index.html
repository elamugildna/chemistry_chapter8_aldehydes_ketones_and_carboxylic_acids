2. Aldol vs Cannizzaro Reaction Comparison

Feature Aldol Condensation Cannizzaro Reaction
Structural Requirement Must have at least one α-hydrogen atom (e.g., CH3CHO, CH3COCH3) Must NOT contain any α-hydrogen atom (e.g., HCHO, C6H5CHO, (CH3)3CCHO)
Reagent / Catalyst Dilute alkali (dil. NaOH, Ba(OH)2) Concentrated alkali (50% NaOH or KOH)
Reaction Nature Enolate formation followed by nucleophilic attack; forms β-hydroxy carbonyl Disproportionation (self oxidation-reduction)
Final Products α,β-unsaturated aldehyde/ketone after dehydration on heating One molecule reduced to Alcohol + one molecule oxidised to Carboxylate salt

3. Relative Acid Strengths of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols because the conjugate base (carboxylate anion) is stabilized by two equivalent resonance structures with negative charge on electronegative oxygen atoms.

CF3COOH > CCl3COOH > CHCl2COOH > NO2CH2COOH > NC-CH2COOH > FCH2COOH > ClCH2COOH > BrCH2COOH > HCOOH > ClCH2CH2COOH > C6H5COOH > C6H5CH2COOH > CH3COOH > CH3CH2COOH

  • EWG (-I / -M effect): -NO2, -CN, -F, -Cl, -Br stabilize the carboxylate anion by dispersing negative charge → increases acid strength.
  • EDG (+I / +M effect): -CH3, -OCH3, -C2H5 destabilize carboxylate anion → decreases acid strength.
  • Benzoic acid exception: Direct attachment of sp2 phenyl ring withdraws electrons inductively, making benzoic acid (pKa 4.19) stronger than acetic acid (pKa 4.76).
